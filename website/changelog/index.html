1.0.0 25 November 2026 Latest

First public release.

A native workbench for Java keystores, PKCS#12 files and X.509 certificates, with a Windows installer and source builds for macOS and Linux. No accounts, no telemetry, MIT licensed.

Download 1.0.0 Release on GitHub

Added

Keystores

  • Six keystore formats. Open, create, edit and save PKCS#12, JKS, JCEKS and Bouncy Castle BKS, UBER and BCFKS files side by side, and convert between all six. Stores without a password open too.
  • Files other tools accept. Output is keytool and OpenSSL compatible, the Bouncy Castle writers reproduce Bouncy Castle's own files byte for byte, and PKCS#12 can be written in a compatibility profile (3DES, SHA-1 MAC) for Java 8 and Windows 7.
  • Editing you can take back. Undo and redo, cut, copy and paste between keystores, rename, labels and a CSV inventory export, with unsaved changes tracked and guarded on close and quit.
  • Locked key pairs. Key pairs open locked and need their key password before they can be inspected, exported, copied or used for signing; Settings can turn this off. Per-key passwords in JKS and JCEKS are preserved.
  • New entries. Generate RSA (2048 to 4096), ECDSA (P-256, P-384, P-521) and Ed25519 key pairs, self-signed or issued by any open CA, and AES, HMAC and 3DES secret keys. Import keys from PEM, DER, OpenSSH, JWK or another keystore, generate CSRs and install CA replies.
  • Build a PKCS#12. Bundle a certificate or chain and its private key into a .p12 or .pfx in one step, from the dashboard or the command palette. The key is matched against the certificate before anything is written.

Certificates

  • A page of their own. Certificate files opened on their own (PEM, DER, chains, PKCS#7 bundles) are listed one row per certificate, with type, algorithm, expiry and health. The list survives restarts, and removing a certificate never changes its file.
  • Export and reuse. Export as PEM, DER, PKCS#7, public key or JWK, copy, or add to any open keystore or workspace. Paste PEM, or keep certificates found in Examine.

Examine

  • Live TLS inspection. Protocol, cipher, ALPN, handshake time and the full chain, with a trust and hostname verdict against your operating system's trust store.
  • Connection test. Says whether a server port can be reached from your machine, and if not, why: refused, timed out, unreachable or not resolved.
  • Decode and compare. Decode anything you paste, from certificates, CSRs and CRLs to keys, PKCS#7, JWTs, OpenSSH keys and keystores, and compare two certificates.
  • Revocation checks. Check a certificate chain with its CA over OCSP or CRL, only when you ask.

CA & Signing

  • Sign CSRs with any CA key pair in an open keystore. The request is verified and its SANs and usages are shown first, and the result installs into the matching key pair in one click.
  • CRLs, CMS and JWTs. Issue v2 CRLs, create and verify CMS/PKCS#7 signatures (attached or detached), and sign and verify JWTs (RS, ES, EdDSA, HS).
  • JAR signing that jarsigner verifies, with RFC 3161 trusted timestamps on CMS and JAR signatures.
  • DH parameters from the RFC 7919 groups or freshly generated safe primes.

Convert

  • Every conversion in one place. Certificates, private and public keys, keystores between all six formats, key and certificate to PKCS#12, keystore to PEM bundle, and PEM to DER and back for any object.
  • The command, shown. Each converter shows the equivalent openssl, ssh-keygen or keytool command, with the Bouncy Castle provider flags where keytool needs them.

Security audit

  • A health score across every open keystore, with findings for expired and expiring certificates, weak keys and digests, deprecated ciphers, incomplete chains and self-signed servers.
  • One-click fixes. Rotate key replaces the key and keeps the subject, SANs and usages; Renew re-issues the certificate with the same key from the open issuing CA. The report exports to CSV.

Vault

  • One master key. Remember keystore passwords under a key derived with Argon2id, each secret sealed on its own with AES-256-GCM.
  • Auto-lock and restore. The vault locks after inactivity, and keystores from your last session reopen once you unlock it. Changing the master key re-encrypts every secret.

Workspaces

  • Group by project or audit scope. Collect keystore entries and certificates into workspaces that persist across sessions.

Productivity

  • Command palette (Ctrl K) reaches every page, action, keystore and entry.
  • Built-in terminal (Ctrl `) with ready-made keytool and openssl commands for the open keystores, and Test-NetConnection on every platform.
  • Tools. Settings → Tools shows which command-line tools the app refers to are installed, with version, path and the install command for any that are missing. The engine needs none of them.
  • The small things. Window-wide drag and drop, keyboard shortcuts, recent files, session restore, and light, dark (true black) or system themes.

Security & quality

  • Interop tests. Keystores, CSRs, CRLs, CMS, JARs, DH parameters and exports are round-tripped through the real keytool, jarsigner, openssl and ssh-keygen, plus a TLS handshake against openssl s_server.
  • Fuzzed parsers. Every parser is fed malformed input (truncations, bit flips, forged length headers, deep nesting) and must return an error rather than crash. Coverage-guided fuzz targets run as a smoke test in CI.
  • A strict Content Security Policy. The interface may load only the app's own scripts and styles and talk only to the Rust engine. It can't reach the internet.
  • Dependency checks. cargo deny checks every Rust dependency for advisories, licences and sources in CI.
  • Safe saves. Saves are written atomically and read back before they replace the original. Keystore contents and passwords stay in memory.

Known limitations

  • Bouncy Castle edge cases. BCFKS stores protected with HMAC-SHA3-512 are refused, PBE-key entries are listed as not shown, a signature-protected BCFKS store is saved back with a password MAC, and UBER store version 0 isn't read.
  • PuTTY .ppk keys aren't read. Convert them with puttygen key.ppk -O private-openssh.
  • PKCS#11 hardware tokens aren't supported: keys on a token can't be exported, which doesn't fit the "a keystore is a file" model.
  • Post-quantum, DSA, Ed448 and RSA-PSS keys load and save losslessly but can't sign. DSA and RSA-PSS signatures on certificates, CSRs and CRLs are verified.
  • Timestamp authorities. The TSA's own certificate chain isn't validated in the app; openssl ts -verify and jarsigner -verify do that.

Road to 1.0.

Thirty-two days from the first commit to the first release, as the commit history tells it.

  1. 24 Oct 2026
    The project starts. The first commit of the repository.
  2. 9 Nov 2026
    xkeystore becomes Keystore Studio. The rename lands across identifiers, titles and metadata, alongside the Vault and Workspaces views with master-key management (#5) and a CI crash fix with better Windows interop (#7).
  3. 10 Nov 2026
    Revocation and trust. OCSP revocation checking and system trust anchors.
  4. 13 Nov 2026
    Undo and redo in the header. New application icons the day before; the UI and OCSP work merges as #8.
  5. 14 Nov 2026
    Monochrome, and tool detection. Design tokens move to monochrome branding, page headers get icons, and Settings learns to detect the command-line tools the app refers to.
  6. 15 Nov 2026
    MD5 fingerprints go. They're no longer shown in certificate details.
  7. 17 Nov 2026
    Formats and help. Broader keystore format support and better help documentation.
  8. 23 Nov 2026
    Title bar and sidebar. The macOS title bar behaves like the Windows one, the collapsed sidebar is refined, and the interop tests check JAR signing more closely.
  9. 25 Nov 2026
    1.0.0 ships. The Windows x64 installer is added and the release is published (#9).
